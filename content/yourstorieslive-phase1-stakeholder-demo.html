From PHP pages
to Next.js.

Release 2.0 moves the member experience onto a modern Next.js interface, with a NestJS BFF connecting it to the existing backend. This briefing tells the story of the migration since the project began on 13 July: what Phase 1 delivered, how the screens connect to the backend, and what remains.

5 of 5 Phase 1 modules builtBFF integration in place27 public pages · 9 languages
Speaker notesWelcome everyone. For the last three months the team has been rebuilding the YourStoriesLive member experience. We did not start from a blank page: the PHP application is the product reference, and our job was to carry its workflows into a modern interface and make them better on the way. Today I will show what we committed to for Phase 1, what is finished, how it connects to the backend, and what is still open. Where I give numbers, they come from the code and the API inventory as of 9 October.

01 · Migration status

A new UI layer.
The backend continues.

The goal is to replace the PHP-rendered experience with Next.js while preserving the established product workflows. The BFF provides the integration boundary between the browser and the existing services, so the new screens never talk to the legacy backend directly.

04 · Phase 1 · Ancestry

Ancestry

The family tree keeps the established workflow and adds a focused mobile view. Members build their tree relative by relative, and can see a contact’s tree read-only.

An interactive family tree

Members see their family as a tree on desktop and as a focused, easier-to-read view on a phone. The page also links to Contacts and to privacy settings so a relative can be turned into a contact.

Add a relative

Add a father, mother, sibling, spouse or child with name, relationship, dates and a photo. Minors can be added with a guardian’s details.

Edit a relative

Any detail can be changed; the form opens with everything already filled in.

Remove a relative

Removing someone asks for confirmation first, and the change is saved to the backend.

A contact’s tree

On a contact’s profile, their tree is shown read-only, and the tab appears only when they have family to show.

Speaker notesAncestry is where a member records who is in their family. On a desktop it is the full interactive tree; on a phone it is a view designed for small screens, because a wide tree does not fit on one. Members can add a father, mother, sibling, spouse or child, edit any of them with the form already filled in, and remove someone after a confirmation. On a contact’s profile you can see their tree, read-only. Two things are not built yet: choosing an existing contact from a dropdown to place them in the tree, and sharing the tree as a link.

05 · Phase 1 · Profile, Reflections & Advice

Profile · Reflections · Advice

Profile is where members tell their life story: sixteen guided questions, important moments, advice for the people they care about, and reflections on the people in their lives, in writing or on camera.

Your Life

Sixteen questions, shown for the life stages a member has reached (childhood, young adult, adult, senior). Each can have several answers with photos, audio or video, and each answer can be edited or deleted.

Skip, bring back and carry forward

A question that does not apply can be turned off and brought back later, and a stage’s answers can be copied forward to the next stage.

Smart suggestions

For books, songs, artists, TV shows and films, actors, restaurants and sports teams, the app offers a match with a cover and details to attach to the answer.

Your Moments

Education (level, institution, years, location, notes) and important dated milestones with photos or videos. They also appear on the timeline.

Your Advice

Advice on topics, written or recorded on camera, with automatic transcripts. Each entry is dated and can be opened, played or deleted.

Reflections

A written, recorded or uploaded memory about a contact, who is notified by email. Members also see reflections others wrote about them and can hide or show each one.

Reading another member’s story

On a contact’s page, their chapters and advice are shown read-only, with resolved names, authors and birthdays.

Speaker notesThis is the heart of the product for most members. Your Life asks sixteen questions, shown only for the life stages the member has reached, so a young member does not see questions meant for later in life. Answers can include photos, audio or video, and members can turn a question off if it does not apply. When someone types a book, a song or a film, the app offers a match with a cover and details. Moments captures education and important dates, which also appear on the timeline. Advice lets a member leave written or recorded advice on topics, with automatic transcripts. Reflections are short memories about a specific person; members can also see what others wrote about them and hide any of it. On a contact’s page the same content is shown read-only. Not built yet: a latest-reflection card on the dashboard.

06 · Phase 1 · Timeline & Stories

Timeline & Stories

The timeline preserves the story lifecycle and adds clear navigation across life stages. Stories can be shared with contacts for comments, and the migration keeps that whole conversation connected.

A timeline of the member’s life

Stories grouped by life stage, with search, filters for content, life stage and tags, a jump-to row, and loading as the member scrolls. On a phone the filters collapse into a window.

The story composer

Tell a story by writing, uploading audio or video, or recording in the browser. Add photos from the library or from Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest, get an automatic transcript, and set the date, life period and place.

Choose who can see it

Public, Private or Secret, with a short explanation of what each means. Stories can be edited or deleted later.

Request comments

Pick contacts, or add a new one on the spot, and allow each to keep a copy. Invitations go out by email.

Comment without an account

An invitee opens the emailed link, reads the story and what others said, then comments with text, photos, video or audio, or declines.

Review and moderate

The owner approves or hides comments, deletes them, asks for a revision that starts from what was already written, or invites someone again. Dashboard lists show invitations and contributions.

Copy and keep a story

A contributor allowed to copy keeps it on their own timeline, with the original author shown. Otherwise they ask the owner, who answers from the email or notification.

Speaker notesThe timeline is the member’s life laid out in order, grouped by life stage, with search and filters and a jump-to row so they can reach a period quickly. Stories can be written, or told by uploading or recording audio or video, with photos, and members choose who can see each one. The collaboration part is what makes this more than a list: the owner invites contacts to comment, an invitee can open the emailed link without an account, comment with text, photos, audio or video, or decline, and the owner can review, hide, ask for a revision or invite again. If the owner allowed it, the invitee can keep a copy on their own timeline; if not, they can ask the owner, who answers from the email.

07 · Phase 1 · Photo Gallery

Photo Gallery

The gallery moves beyond a simple upload list: it keeps media connected to stories, contacts and sharing workflows.

The library

Photos and videos grouped by month, with sort by date, filters and search. Counts and month headings match exactly what can be shown.

Photo details

Open a photo to see and edit who, when, where and caption, and the stories it appears in. One caption can be applied to a whole selection.

Upload and import

Large files upload in parts. Photos can be imported from Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest, and recordings get an automatic transcript.

Connect photos to stories and contacts

Add a selection to a story or start a new story from it, attach photos to a contact, or create a contact from the dialog.

Delete with care

Before a photo is deleted the member sees which stories and contacts it is linked to.

Share and request

Share with contacts; approve or deny what is “Shared with you” and what is “Requested from you”; request photos from another member.

Another member’s photos

“Organized groups” tiles, search by story name, life-stage filter, group by stories, sort, and a viewer showing when each photo was added, taken and where.

Photo comments

Visitors can comment on a photo, and owners see every comment on their own photos and can hide or show each one; a hidden comment stays listed, dimmed.

Speaker notesPhoto Gallery goes well beyond an upload list. Photos are shown by month and can be searched, filtered and sorted. Members can upload large files in parts, or import from Facebook, Instagram and Pinterest, then caption photos, attach them to a story or to a contact, and share them with other members. Before a photo is deleted, the member is shown exactly what it is linked to. Sharing works in both directions: photos shared with you, and photos other members have requested from you, each with Approve and Deny. On another member’s profile you can browse their photos, narrow them down, and request photos from them. Comments on photos can be hidden or shown by the owner.

08 · Phase 1 · Profile Completion

Profile Completion

Profile completion now guides members to missing required details, and shows how far through their life story they are.

Required-details gate

A member needs a name, email, date of birth and phone number to use most of the app. If something is missing they are taken to Settings with a clear message.

Personal information

Edit name, date of birth, phone, bio and social links, plus optional gender, race and ethnicity. A first save creates the profile.

Profile photo

Upload a photo with a square crop, or remove it.

Completion and progress

The profile shows an overall completion percentage and a progress bar for each life stage, and dashboard prompts point to the next thing to do.

Speaker notesA profile that is missing basics causes problems elsewhere in the product, so we made completion explicit. A member needs a name, email, date of birth and phone number before they can use most of the app; if something is missing they are taken to Settings with a clear message. Once those are filled in, the profile page shows an overall completion percentage and a progress bar for each life stage, and the dashboard points to the next thing to do. Saving a postal address is the one piece not built yet.

09 · Authentication

One session contract
across login methods.

Members can sign in many ways. Whichever way they arrive, the new application gives them the same kind of session.

AuthenticatePassword, social, email/SMS code or invitation link
→
Map legacy responseBFF translates PHP fields into the Next.js contract
→
Issue BFF sessionAccess and refresh tokens preserve the account identity
→
ContinueRefresh restores a valid session and destination
⇢

Legacy compatibility

PHP session credentials are retained where needed while the browser uses the BFF-issued API session.

◉

Identity continuity

The account identity is carried through sign-in, sign-up, social, OTP and contribution-token authentication.

⌕

Traceable handoff

The BFF logs the captured account ID for verification without logging session credentials.

Why the BFF mattersIt gives Next.js a consistent authentication contract while the existing PHP APIs remain behind the migration boundary.
Speaker notesMembers can come in with an email and password, with Google, Facebook, Kakao or Naver, with a verification code by SMS or email, or through an invitation link. The PHP backend returns data for each of these in its own format. The BFF translates all of them into one consistent session, so the interface does not need to know which route a member took. A quiet refresh keeps them signed in, and if a session does expire they are returned to where they were. This is also where the BFF earns its place: the old backend stays behind it while the browser only ever speaks one contract.

10 · Beyond Phase 1 · Accounts, Settings & Contacts

Also complete:
accounts and contacts.

Beyond the five Phase 1 modules, the supporting pages that members rely on every day are also built.

Sign-up, sign-in and passwords

Email and password with an optional second step by SMS or email code; sign-in with Google, Facebook, Kakao or Naver; forgot-password with a code; quiet session refresh; a password prompt for people who join by invitation.

Settings

Hide from search, choose the default audience for new stories, turn on two-step verification, change the password, and see the account’s guardian status and administrator (executor).

Contacts

A searchable, sortable list with a relationship filter; add with a photo, edit and delete; set an access level; ask for, approve, reject or revoke access; tabs for stories commented, photos, deliveries and reflections; and Request Comments for chosen stories.

Find people and visit their page

The header search finds a member by name or email, with View and Connect. A member’s page shows Timeline, Chapters, Advice, Ancestry, Photos and Reflections, with filters, photo requests, comments and “add a reflection”.

Speaker notesThese pages are outside the Phase 1 list but the product needs them. Accounts cover sign-up, sign-in, password reset and two-step verification. Settings lets members decide who can find them and who can see their stories by default, change their password, and see who is their administrator. Contacts is a full page: search, sort, add with a photo, edit, delete, set the access level, and ask for or grant access, plus asking a contact to comment on selected stories. Header search lets a member find another member by name or email and open their page; it works on larger screens today. Not built yet: appointing an executor, and adding a person met on another member’s page to your contacts.

Notifications now open as the email they were sent as, are marked read as you open them, and members can mark everything read or clear it. The welcome slides appear once after sign-up. Contact us is the one public page that talks to the backend, sending a message to the team.

Speaker notesI want to be open about what is not done. The largest items are whole areas that need backend work first: Gifts and Messages, Concierge and Billing. Then there are product decisions: search currently shows an email and birth date for each result, which we restored to match the old console but which exposes personal information, and “Ask to view” does nothing yet. A few settings features, like appointing an executor, are not available. There is also polish: a few save and delete failures on the profile give no message to the member. And four Phase 1 screens are pending: placing a contact in the family tree, sharing the tree, a latest-reflection card on the dashboard, and saving an address.
